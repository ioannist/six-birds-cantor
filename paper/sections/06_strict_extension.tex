\section{The split pair: strict extension and growth blindness}\label{sec:extension}

This section constructs two shell states that the growth theory cannot tell apart but the completion theory can, and proves Theorems~\ref{thm:strict-extension} and~\ref{thm:blindness}.

\subsection{Two transposed kernels}

Start from the uniform kernel $J$. For each triple $(i,j,a)$ in
\[
(0,3,\tfrac1{10000}),\;(1,3,-\tfrac1{20000}),\;(2,3,-\tfrac1{40000}),\;
(4,7,\tfrac1{12500}),\;(5,7,\tfrac3{50000}),\;(6,7,\tfrac1{25000}),
\]
add $a$ to the diagonal entries $(i,i)$ and $(j,j)$ and subtract $a$ from the off-diagonal entries $(i,j)$ and $(j,i)$. Call the resulting symmetric matrix~$S_0$. These \emph{tags} give the states $0,\dots,7$ distinct diagonal values. Then add $\delta=10^{-6}$ on the directed cycle $0\to1\to2\to0$ and subtract $\delta$ on the reverse cycle:
\[
\Kp=S_0+\delta\,(E_{01}+E_{12}+E_{20}-E_{10}-E_{21}-E_{02}),\qquad \Km=\Kp^{\top},
\]
where $E_{ij}$ is the matrix unit. All entries are rational and are listed in Appendix~\ref{app:witness-data}.

\begin{lemma}[The warm kernels]\label{lem:warm-kernels}
The kernels $\Kp$ and $\Km$ have the following properties.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Both are positive and doubly stochastic, and each row gives mass $1/2$ to each half $\{0,\dots,9\}$ and $\{10,\dots,19\}$.
  \item For every $i$, row $i$ of $\Km$ is a permutation of row $i$ of $\Kp$. For $i\notin\{0,1,2\}$ the rows are identical; for $i\in\{0,1,2\}$ two entries $\tfrac1{20}\pm\delta$ are exchanged.
  \item $(\Kp)_{ij}=(\Km)_{ij}$ whenever $j\notin\{0,1,2\}$.
  \item Both have the same diagonal. Their finite informants are exactly uniform, so their supports, prototype weights and audit cells coincide. The audit core is $\{0,4,5,6,7\}$, and the reinstatement matrices $U_{\mathrm{audit}}$ are equal.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Items 1--3 are direct checks of the construction. Every tag preserves row and column sums and is symmetric. The cycle term has zero row and column sums. The cycle term is also antisymmetric, so transposition reverses the direction of the cycle; in rows $0,1,2$ it exchanges the entries $\tfrac1{20}+\delta$ and $\tfrac1{20}-\delta$, which lie in columns $\{0,1,2\}$. For item~4, a doubly stochastic kernel fixes the uniform vector, so the power iteration stops after its first step at the uniform vector. The support $\kappa_i=\tfrac12(\tfrac1{20}+K_{ii})$ then depends only on the diagonal. The five largest diagonal values belong to the states $7,0,4,5,6$.
\end{proof}

The \emph{warm states} $\xp$ and $\xm$ have kernels $\Kp$ and $\Km$, phase $0$, $\tau=3/5$, budget $12$, the refresh protocol, and incumbent audit lens and budget packaging. Their selector records also contain one previous selection, the audit lens and the budget packaging. The lens and packaging switches at the joining step therefore incur both switch penalties, which reset the timescale to $0.6$. Each carries an admissible future in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:shell}: a joining step to a common kernel $R$, the entry prefix, and a parameter word in $\Cr^{\N}$. The future after the joining step is the same for both.

\subsection{The completion objects differ}

\begin{proposition}[Distinct completion objects]\label{prop:distinct-objects}
The audit completion maps $E_{\xp}$ and $E_{\xm}$ are positive stochastic matrices with a common reinstatement matrix and exactly computable rational stationary vectors $\Gamma_\pm=\Gamma(x_\pm)$. These vectors differ. In particular,
\[
(\Gamma_+)_0-(\Gamma_-)_0=\frac{390467997745347846870000}{120131948909489644812213124707327077}\approx3.25\cdot10^{-12}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:warm-kernels}, $E_{x_\pm}=B_{3/5}(K_\pm)\,U$ with a common $U$. The vectors $\Gamma_\pm$ are computed in exact rational arithmetic. Stationarity, positivity and unit mass are verified exactly, as is the displayed nonzero difference. By Lemma~\ref{lem:completion-object} each is the unique fixed point and attracts every initial probability vector. These facts are checked in Lean (Section~\ref{sec:verification}).
\end{proof}

The difference is small but exact: it is a rational number, not a floating-point separation. It arises because $B_{3/5}(K_\pm)$ carries the direction of the cycle into the audit cell $\{0,4,5,6,7\}$, which contains state~$0$ but not states $1,2$.

\subsection{The joining step}

From either warm state, the scheduled selection at phase~$0$ picks the spectral lens, with score $L=37/80$, and the cluster packaging, with score $0.736+0.035\sqrt3\in(0.79,0.80)$. Both are isolated beyond hysteresis and tie band. The physical cluster packaging takes all twenty states as its core. Its two groups are the two halves of $[d]$. This physical grouping is different from the audit completion cell above, and the construction never identifies the two. Let $R$ be the fixed rational kernel obtained by averaging the rows of the pre-innovation operator within each half $\{0,\dots,9\}$ and $\{10,\dots,19\}$, computed at the rational construction score $159/200$. At the warm states the pre-innovation operator is an affine function of the kernel, $W(K)=0.65\,[(1-\eta)K+\eta\,T(K)]+0.35\,J$ with $T$ affine, because the physical core is all of~$[d]$. The tag and cycle perturbations have zero column sums within each half, so the averaging erases them and gives the same $R$ for both kernels. $R$ is doubly stochastic, and its rows are equal within each half. Choose the innovations $N_\pm=R-W(x_\pm)$. Exact endpoint checks, which use the actual irrational packaging score and not a rational substitute, show that $|N_\pm|<6\cdot10^{-4}$ entrywise, well below the legal amplitude $2.5\cdot10^{-3}$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:target-control}, both states move to kernel~$R$.

All scalars that enter the next state and the observable agree for $\xp$ and $\xm$: the selected scores, the P1 target distance, the P2 viabilities, the pre-innovation variation, the timescale and budget updates, and the action entropy. These scalars are invariant under transposition of the kernel. The final variations agree as well, because the cycle term is orthogonal, in the Frobenius inner product, both to $R$ (whose rows $0,1,2$ coincide, while the cycle term has zero column sums) and to the symmetric matrix~$S_0$. Hence
$q(\xp)=q(\xm)$ for both observables, and $F(\xp)$ and $F(\xm)$ agree in every coordinate read by $F$ and~$q$.
After the joining step the two states follow the same certified entry prefix and the same parameter word.

\subsection{The history identity}

\begin{lemma}[Common rows erase supported differences]\label{lem:common-rows}
Let $A$ and $B$ be $d\times d$ matrices with equal row sums, and suppose $A_{ij}=B_{ij}$ for all $j\notin I$. If $M$ is a $d\times d$ matrix whose rows indexed by $I$ are all equal, then $AM=BM$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $m$ be the common row of $M$ on~$I$. Row $i$ of $(A-B)M$ is $\sum_{j\in I}(A-B)_{ij}\,m=\bigl(\sum_j(A-B)_{ij}\bigr)m=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[History identity]\label{prop:history-identity}
Fix either observable, any $s\ge0$ and the common future of the warm states. Then
\[
\sum_j A_s(\xp)_{ij}=\sum_j A_s(\xm)_{ij}\ \text{ for every } i,
\qquad
C_{s,n}(\xp)=C_{s,n}(\xm)\ \text{ for every } n\ge2.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $q_0=q(x_\pm)$, which is common to both states. Then $A_s(x_\pm)_{ij}=(e^{-q_0}(K_\pm)_{ij})^s$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:warm-kernels}(2) the rows of the two weighted matrices are permutations of each other, so their row sums agree. By Lemma~\ref{lem:warm-kernels}(3) they differ only in columns $I=\{0,1,2\}$. The next weighted matrix, with entries $A_s(Fx_\pm)_{ij}=(e^{-q_1}R_{ij})^s$, is common and has equal rows on $I$, because the rows of $R$ are equal within the first half. By Lemma~\ref{lem:common-rows}, $A_s(\xp)A_s(F\xp)=A_s(\xm)A_s(F\xm)$. Every later factor is common, since the later states agree in every coordinate read by $F$ and~$q$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The two theorems}

\begin{theorem}[Strict extension]\label{thm:strict-extension}
The warm states satisfy $\xp,\xm\in\Sh$, $\Desc(\xp)=\Desc(\xm)$ and $\Gamma(\xp)\ne\Gamma(\xm)$. Consequently the completion object does not factor through the base description on~$\Sh$, and the hybrid object $\Hyb=(\Desc,\Gamma)$ is a strict retentive extension of the growth theory.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The warm states and their futures belong to $\Sh$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:shell}. Items~1 and~2 of Definition~\ref{def:base} agree by construction and Lemma~\ref{lem:warm-kernels}(4). For item~3, Proposition~\ref{prop:history-identity} gives equal row sums at $n=1$, hence equal maximum row sums. It gives equal matrices for $n\ge2$, and $C_{s,0}=I$. So the norms agree at every horizon, for every $s\ge0$ and for both observables. Finally, $\Gamma(\xp)\ne\Gamma(\xm)$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:distinct-objects}, and Definition~\ref{def:factorization} applies.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:strict-extension} does not say that the completion object is undefinable from \emph{all} data at the state. The labelled kernel determines it. What it says is that the specified growth description does not: no function of everything the growth theory records reconstructs~$\Gamma$.

\begin{theorem}[Growth blindness]\label{thm:blindness}
For both observables, every $s\ge0$ and every admissible common future of the warm states:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the weighted history matrices $C_{s,n}(\xp)$ and $C_{s,n}(\xm)$ coincide for every $n\ge2$, and their norms coincide for every $n\ge0$;
  \item for every probability vector $u$ with full support, $u\,C_{s,n}(\xp)\one$ and $u\,C_{s,n}(\xm)\one$ differ by at most a factor $1/\min_ju_j$ at every horizon. In particular, their exponential growth rates (upper and lower) coincide, and this holds even when the initial vectors are the two distinct completion objects themselves;
  \item every pressure computed from these growth observations, whether a limit, a $\limsup$ or a $\liminf$ of $\frac1n\log\norm{C_{s,n}}$, takes the same value at $\xp$ and at $\xm$, although $\Gamma(\xp)\ne\Gamma(\xm)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Item~1 is Proposition~\ref{prop:history-identity} together with the norm argument in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:strict-extension}. For item~2, a nonnegative matrix $C$ and a probability vector $u$ with $c=\min_ju_j>0$ satisfy $c\norm{C}\le u\,C\one\le\norm{C}$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:completion-object} the completion objects have full support. Item~3 follows from item~1.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Saturation, refinement and lumpability]\label{rem:refinement}
Three notions from the completion literature have exact counterparts at the warm states.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Saturation.} Each completion map is a strict contraction (Lemma~\ref{lem:completion-object}), so repeated completion converges to its unique object.
  \item \emph{Refinement.} Switching the completion lens from audit to spectral produces, at the same kernel and timescale, a new object $p_{\mathrm{spec}}$. Because the spectral core is all of~$[d]$, this object is the same for both kernels and differs from both audit objects. The row-similarity lens has singleton cells, so its object is the uniform vector~$u$. The unordered families $\{\Gamma_+,p_{\mathrm{spec}},u\}$ and $\{\Gamma_-,p_{\mathrm{spec}},u\}$ are therefore different, while the spectral output alone is shared and does not witness strictness.
  \item \emph{Lumpability.} The audit transport is not lumpable with respect to its two cells; its exact lumpability defect is $101/3906250$ \cite{KemenySnell1976FiniteMarkovChains}. So the cell masses do not evolve by a closed macro dynamics.
\end{itemize}
None of these facts is needed for Theorems~\ref{thm:strict-extension} and~\ref{thm:blindness}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Strictness is information at the current state]
After the joining step the two physical trajectories coincide, so the distinction between $\Gamma_+$ and $\Gamma_-$ need not survive along the outer dynamics. Theorem~\ref{thm:strict-extension} is a statement about the information available at the current state. It does not assert a permanent difference between future physical trajectories.
\end{remark}
